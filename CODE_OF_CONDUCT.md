# Code of Conduct

**Status: draft for the technical preview.**

## Our commitment

Everyone who takes part in OperateBench — contributing, reviewing, reporting a
defect, or arguing that the construct is wrong — should be able to do so without
harassment, and should expect to be judged on the substance of their work.

We want this project to be a place where "your benchmark does not measure what
you claim" is a normal, welcome sentence, said and received without personal
attack in either direction.

## Expected behaviour

- Engage with the argument, not the person making it.
- Give and accept technical criticism directly and specifically. A rejected
  design is not a rejected contributor.
- Say what you actually think about evidence, including when it undercuts the
  project's own claims.
- Be explicit about uncertainty rather than confident by default.
- Respect other people's time: search first, include reproduction steps, keep
  scope tight.
- Assume good faith, and ask before concluding someone acted badly.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, stalking or sustained disruption.
- Insults, demeaning comments, or attacks based on personal characteristics —
  including age, disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, level of
  experience, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual
  identity and orientation.
- Sexualised language, imagery or attention in any project space.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately submitting content the contributor knows to be non-synthetic,
  personal or proprietary, or knowingly misrepresenting evidence, provenance or
  results.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern in good faith.

## Scope

This applies in all project spaces — the repository, issues, pull requests,
discussions and reviews — and when someone is representing the project in public.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report to **dmitry@dwelly.group**. Reports are handled confidentially, and the
reporter's identity is not shared with the person reported without the
reporter's consent.

Reports are acknowledged within 3 working days where possible. A maintainer with
a conflict of interest in a report — including one who is its subject — recuses
themselves from handling it.

Responses are proportionate, and typically escalate: a private explanation of
what was wrong; a warning; a temporary restriction from project spaces; or a
permanent ban. Where a response is applied, the reasoning is recorded, and the
person affected can respond.

Governance during the preview is small and informal: Dwelly is the initial
steward and currently makes these decisions. That is stated plainly rather than
dressed up as a committee, and it will be revisited if the project grows.
